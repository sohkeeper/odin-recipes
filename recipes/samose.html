SAMOSE

Making classic potato samosas involves preparing a stiff, flaky dough, a spiced potato filling, shaping them into cones, and frying them at a low temperature

MAKING THE SAMOSA DOUGH

  • Combine all-purpose flour (maida), ajwain (carom seeds), salt, and oil or ghee.
  • Rub the fat into the flour thoroughly with your fingers until the mixture resembles coarse breadcrumbs or sand to ensure a flaky crust.
  • Add water gradually to form a stiff, tight dough rather than a soft roti dough.
  • Cover the dough with a damp cloth and let it rest for 20 to 30 minutes

PREPARING THE FILLING

  1. Boil, peel, and roughly mash potatoes, keeping some small chunks for texture.
  2. Sauté cumin seeds, ginger, and green chilies in a pan with a little oil until fragrant.
  3. Add green peas, the mashed potatoes, and dry spices like coriander powder, red chili powder, amchur (mango powder), and garam masala.
  4. Stir well to combine, let the mixture cool completely, and according to Quora, “ensure the masala is not too fluidy.

SHAPING AND FOLDING

  1. Divide the rested dough into equal portions and roll each ball into a thin oval or circle.
  2. Cut the rolled piece cleanly in half across the middle.
  3. Brush the straight edge of the half-circle with water, bring the straight ends together, and overlap them to form a neat cone.
  4. Pack a small spoonful of the cooled potato filling inside without overstuffing.
  5. Apply water along the top inside edge and pinch tightly to seal the seam closed

FRYING THE SAMOSA

  1. Heat cooking oil in a deep pan on medium, then lower the temperature before dropping the samosas in.
  2. Fry the samosas slowly on low to medium-low heat so the crust cooks evenly and stays crisp without bubbling or getting greasy.
  3. Turn them frequently until they turn an even, golden-brown shade.
